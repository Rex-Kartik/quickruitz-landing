A  single reference document covering QuickRuit's origin, vision, product, positioning, business model, and pitch.
Written to be understood equally well by a human reading it once, or an AI model using it as context.

1. Origin Story — Why I Built QuickRuit

QuickRuit didn't start with domain expertise. It started with a question.

During his fourth semester, founder Rahul Shrivastwa was actively applying for internships — building profiles on
LinkedIn,  Internshala,  Naukri,  optimizing  his  resume,  emailing  HRs,  applying  to  hundreds  of  opportunities.  He
wasn't a topper, but he was a solid candidate: 80-85% scores, real projects, relevant skills. Yet many applications
went unanswered.

At first he assumed the problem was competition. Then he looked at it from the other side: if he was applying to
hundreds  of  jobs,  recruiters  were  receiving  thousands  of  applications  for  each  one.  A  single  recruiter  had  to
review resumes, compare profiles, schedule interviews, and repeat the same screening questions over and over,
trying to find the right few people in an overwhelming pile.

The  question  changed  from  "Why  am  I  not  getting  selected?"  to  "How  does  a  recruiter  find the right candidate
among  thousands  of  applications?"  —  and  then  to:  What  if  recruiters  didn't  have  to  start  with  thousands  of
resumes?  What  if  they  could  start  with  the  top  5%  most  relevant  candidates  instead?  What  if  communication,
practical  understanding,  and  role  fit  could  be  assessed  automatically,  before  a  recruiter  even  opened  the  first
application?

The  real problem wasn't a shortage of talent. It was filtering talent at scale — and the first round of hiring was
still almost entirely manual, on both sides of the table. That insight became QuickRuit.

2. Vision & Mission

Vision: AI handles the first round. Humans handle the final decision.

Mission:  Remove  the  repetitive,  manual  first-round  work  in  hiring — resume filtering, scheduling, repeating the
same  screening  questions  —  so  recruiters  spend  their  time  on  what  actually  needs  human  judgment:
relationship-building and final hiring decisions.

QuickRuit  does  not aim to replace recruiters. It aims to remove the operations that shouldn't require a human in
the first place.

3. The Problem

●  A single job opening in India can receive 300-500 applications within days; usually only 15-20 of those are

genuinely relevant.

●  Recruiters  still  manually  review  nearly  every  profile  —  100%  human  effort  for  roughly  20%  useful

candidates.

●  Recruiters report spending 20-30 hours screening candidates for a single role.
●  Traditional  ATS  platforms  store  and  organize  resumes,  but  do  not  evaluate  candidates  —  they  solve

storage, not filtering.

●  Resume claims are self-reported and unverified; a meaningful share contain misleading or inflated claims,

and recruiters still have to manually verify everything.

●  The  underlying  problem  isn't  a  shortage  of candidates. It's that companies have no fast, scalable way to

know who is actually capable before committing recruiter and hiring-manager time.

4. What QuickRuit Actually Is (Positioning — Final, Locked)

Do not describe QuickRuit primarily as: a resume screening tool, a sourcing platform, a job board, a traditional
ATS, or a generic "AI-powered hiring platform." These are either what QuickRuit is not, or phrases so overused by
every competitor that they carry no meaning anymore.

The core mechanism: A resume is a claim. A single interview is mostly a guess. QuickRuit generates evidence
instead  —  through  adaptive  assessments,  dynamic  AI  interviews,  and  (for  technical  roles)  coding/reasoning
rounds — and compiles all of it into one explainable scorecard before a recruiter ever schedules a call.

The  core  outcome  (what  people  actually  want,  and  what  should  lead  every  pitch):  Recruiters  get  a
trustworthy shortlist in hours instead of weeks, because AI does the repetitive first-round work.

Rule  for  every  pitch,  deck,  or  landing  page  copy:  lead  with  the  outcome  (time  saved),  back  it  with  the
mechanism  (evidence-based,  not a black box). Neither alone is enough — "automation" alone is generic and
every competitor says it; "evidence" alone is abstract without the time/effort payoff attached.

backed by evidence, not a black-box score."

5. The Product — What QuickRuit Does

Current workflow (as it exists today)

1.  Job creation — company creates a job opening / job description.
2.  Resume  parsing  &  matching  —  AI  parses  resumes  and  evaluates  candidates  against  the  role  using

skills, experience, and (for technical roles) live GitHub/LeetCode data.

3.  Async  AI video interview — candidates interview anytime, from any device, no scheduling coordination

needed. This removes a major operational bottleneck for both sides.

4.  AI  evaluation  —  while/after  the  interview,  AI  evaluates  communication,  technical  understanding,  and

behavioral signals.

5.  Explainable  scorecard  —  instead  of  a  raw,  unexplained  number,  QuickRuit  generates  a  scorecard
recruiters  can  actually  read:  why  a  candidate  scored  high,  where  they  were  weak,  backed  by
transcript/evidence.

6.  Recruiter decision — the recruiter reviews the ranked, evidence-backed shortlist and makes the final call.

QuickRuit does not make hiring decisions.

Product  modules  (be  honest  about  maturity  —  do  not  present  all  of  these  as  equally
finished)

●  Aptitude assessment — initial reasoning/aptitude evaluation layer.
●  Adaptive  technical/role  assessment  —  difficulty  adjusts  based  on  candidate  performance  (stronger
candidate  →  harder  questions);  multiple  question  formats  (MCQ,  multi-select, fill-in-the-blank, one-word,
written). Currently strongest for technical roles.

●  AI  video  interview  —  currently  more  structured/static;  the  product  direction  is  toward  fully  dynamic
interviews  where  the  AI  asks  real  follow-up  questions  based  on  the  candidate's previous answer, rather
than a fixed script. This is a near-term differentiator, not yet fully mature for every role type.

●  Coding/technical  round  —  coding  assessment  today;  the  roadmap  direction  is  an  AI-assisted  coding
interview  that  evaluates  reasoning  and  process,  not  just  final  output,  without becoming a tool that helps
candidates cheat.

●  Bulk screening — employer uploads many resumes at once; AI parses, scores, and ranks against the JD.
●  Campus Drive Mode — bulk assessment mode built for university placement drives.
●  Anti-cheat  /  proctoring  —  signals  like  tab-switching,  gaze  deviation,  and  script-reading  detection  to

protect interview integrity.

Honest gaps (say this clearly, don't hide it)

●  ATS integration is not built yet. It is on the roadmap, not a current feature. Never present it as available

today, in a pitch or on the website — say "coming soon" / "on our roadmap" explicitly.

●  Non-technical  role  evaluation  (sales,  ops,  HR,  finance)  is  a  real  direction  but  not  equally  mature  as

technical roles yet; the honest current strength is technical and select knowledge-based roles.

7. Business Model & Pricing

Model: Pay-as-you-grow, usage-based, low friction — no large upfront commitment required.

●  Free pilot — companies test the product on a real open role before paying anything.
●  Pay-per-interview — ₹99 per AI interview for startups/SMEs (e.g., 100 candidates ≈ ₹9,900 — less than a

week of a recruiter's salary, delivered in ~48 hours instead of weeks).

●  Campus / bulk drives — flat packs starting at ₹4,999 for university placement drives (500+ students per

drive).

●  Unit  economics:  Backend  (Node.js/FastAPI/Redis,  Gemini-based  AI)  is  decoupled  and  lean  —  actual
per-candidate processing cost is roughly ₹5-10, supporting a high (~80%+) gross margin business at these
price points.

(Note: an earlier draft of the pricing also explored flat monthly SaaS tiers — ₹1,999 / ₹4,999 / ₹9,999 per month.
The  usage-based,  pay-per-interview  +  campus-pack  model  above  is  the  current  locked  direction  per  the  latest

pitch  script;  if  the  tiered  model  is  revived  later,  only  one  model  should  be  presented publicly at a time to avoid
investor/customer confusion.)

Who pays, for what:

●  Startups/SMEs & staffing agencies → pay per interview credit, scaling with hiring volume.
●  Universities/TPOs → pay per placement drive, evaluating hundreds of students at once.
●  Corporate HR (later stage) → higher-volume usage or negotiated packages as needs grow.

8. Go-To-Market — Segment by Segment

QuickRuit serves three segments that share the same underlying question — "is this person actually ready for this
opportunity?" — with different entry points and outputs:

●  Corporate  HR  (B2B):  Candidate  →  Evidence  →  Shortlist.  Reduce  manual  first-round  workload,  give

recruiters an explainable, defensible shortlist.

●  Staffing/recruitment  agencies  (B2B):  Large  candidate  pool  →  Evidence  →  Ranked,  client-ready
candidates. Agencies can hand clients evidence, not just a name, which reduces client rejection rates.
●  Universities  /  campus  placement  cells  (B2B2C):  Student  pool  →  Assessment  →  Skill-gap  →

Job-readiness insight. The university/TPO is the buyer; students are the end users being evaluated.

Go-to-market motion:

1.  Product-led growth via free pilots — let hiring teams experience the time savings directly before paying.
2.  Direct B2B outreach to startup/SME HR teams and staffing agency owners.
3.  TPO and university partnerships for campus placement drives.

13. What NOT to Say (Guardrails)

●  Do not claim ATS integration exists today — it's roadmap, say so explicitly.
●  Do not say "first ever," "no competitors," or "we're replacing recruiters."
●  Do  not  present a single, specific efficiency percentage (e.g., "90% faster") without being ready to explain

how it was measured — keep such claims directional and honest.

●  Do  not  blindly  answer  a  technical question you're unsure of — be transparent ("let me confirm the exact

number and follow up") rather than fabricating a precise answer on the spot.

●  Do not claim features that are still in development as fully live, in any pitch, deck, or website copy.
●  Do not build or claim personality/culture-fit prediction from video — legally risky and not part of the current

honest product.
