Ask three different hiring managers to interview the same candidate, and you will likely get three completely different scores. This isn't a flaw in the candidate; it's a critical flaw in human-led, unstructured interviewing.

Welcome to the chaotic world of "Gut-Feel" hiring.

## The 3x Variance Problem

When interviews are not standardized, subjectivity takes over. One interviewer might score a candidate a 42, another a 78, and a third a 91. This **+3x variance** happens because humans are influenced by unconscious biases:

- Did the candidate go to the same college?
- Did they have good small talk before the interview started?
- Was the interviewer just having a bad morning?

## The Tragic Result of Confused Decisions

When scores are inconsistent, the hiring committee cannot make a data-driven decision. The results are damaging to your business:

- **Strong candidates get rejected** because they didn't "vibe" with one specific interviewer.
- **Average candidates get through** simply because they are highly charismatic or confidently wrong.

Can we truly trust business-critical decisions to a process this flawed?
