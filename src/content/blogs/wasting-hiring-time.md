Time is money. In business, this is an accepted truth. Yet, when it comes to hiring, companies seem willing to burn both. Recruitment is one of the biggest drivers of operational time waste and cost in corporate environments. The current status quo isn't just inefficient; it’s expensive.

The 23-hour statistic is shocking, but it’s real. That's nearly three full workdays spent just skimming CVs.

## The Magnitude of Time Waste in Hiring

Let’s break down where the time goes:

- **Initial Sourcing (6+ Hours):** Posting to multiple job boards, LinkedIn outreach, and managing the intake.
- **Manual Resume Screening (23+ Hours):** The absolute biggest drain. Reading 500 CVs for 30 seconds each takes nearly 4 hours — and that’s a conservative estimate.
- **Interview Scheduling (3-5 Hours):** The back-and-forth email shuffle to coordinate calendars between candidates and busy hiring managers.
- **Phone Screens (10+ Hours):** Conducting dozens of 15-minute introductory calls, many with candidates who are clearly not a fit within the first two minutes.

## The Cost of Slow Hiring

The time waste is severe, but the resulting business costs are catastrophic.

### 1. Cost of Vacancy

Every day a critical role is empty, your company is losing revenue or productivity. If a role that generates ₹50,000 in daily productivity remains empty for 30 extra days because your process is slow, that's a ₹15 Lakh opportunity loss.

### 2. Sourcing Costs & Recruiter Burnout

The more time spent on screening, the less time is spent on proactive sourcing or candidate relationship building. Recruiters drown in volume, leading to high burnout and churn rates.

### 3. Hiring Misery

A slow process means bad candidate experience. Top talent will not wait around. You are left choosing from the 'leftovers' rather than the best, which significantly increases the risk of a mis-hire (which costs 2.5x the salary of the role).
